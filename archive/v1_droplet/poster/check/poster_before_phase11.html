1The question

Can a model trained on 12 laser-textured surfaces predict how far a droplet spreads on a surface it has never seen?

Models of droplet impact are usually scored on random train/test splits, where every surface appears in training. Surface design needs the opposite: predictions for a surface before it is made and tested.

Open dataset of Može et al. [1]: FDPA-coated aluminium 1050A, square grids of laser channels, 5 000 fps imaging. Target: maximum spreading factor βmax = Dmax/D0, the one outcome measured on both surface types.

2Physics inputs

Re = ρVD0μ
We = ρV2D0σ
Oh = √WeRe

Recomputed Re and We match the dataset to 5×10−7. Because Oh is a function of Re and We, the dimensionless set carries two groups, not three.

Classic scaling laws fit this superhydrophobic data poorly: Laan et al. [3] gives R² = 0.71. They were derived on wettable surfaces, so we use them as a check, not as the model.

3Reading the surface from SEM

The plateaus between laser tracks are the same smooth surface as REF-H [1]. Segmenting 43× images by local texture gives track widths w ≈ 45 µm (deep) and 30 µm (shallow), so every surface gets a smooth-plateau fraction:

φ = ( max(s − w, 0) / s )2,   Vtex = (1 − φ) h

Textured surfaces span φ = 0.01 (D50) to 0.93 (S800). REF-H is simply φ = 1: a physical value, not a made-up spacing or depth.

4Leakage-safe evaluation

Each condition was repeated 5×. A random split puts near-identical twins in train and test and flatters every model.

Gaussian process regression (Matérn 3/2, one length-scale per input) generalizes best to unseen surfaces: 29% lower error than XGBoost on the same inputs. Kernel ridge and SVR fit well in-distribution (≈0.045) but collapse on unseen surfaces. Every model's worst surface is D50, the densest texture.

6Why the descriptor matters

A smooth plate has no channel spacing or depth. Encoding REF-H with invented values (0/0, 50/6, 800/25 µm…) moved its error by up to 0.022. With φ = 1, changing the measured track width by ±10 µm moves it by at most 0.005.

Geometry is not negligible: removing surface inputs nearly doubles in-distribution error (0.086 vs 0.051). Deep, dense textures spread further.

7Knowing when not to trust it

Isolation Forest + Mahalanobis distance flag D50 and S50, the densest textures, which are hardest to predict.

REF-H is not flagged but has the largest error. Its shift is likely in wetting chemistry (≈114° for a similar coating on smooth Al vs 160–166° textured), which no input describes.

9From an SEM image to a prediction

A small CNN reads φ from 1.2 mm SEM patches. Each surface is scored by a network that never saw it.

Feeding CNN-read φ to the GPR: unseen-surface RMSE 0.043 vs 0.039 with measured φ.

REF-H is read as φ = 0.69 rather than 1.0, because no training surface is smoother than φ = 0.93. Image-derived descriptors work within the texture family they were trained on.

★Take-aways

  1. Judge droplet models on unseen surfaces. Random splits hide the gap.
  2. Describe surfaces with quantities every surface has. The smooth-plateau fraction φ makes the smooth surface a limit of the textured family.
  3. A Gaussian process on physics inputs beats 12 alternatives, including boosted trees and a transformer, and predicts the smooth surface to 1.9%.

Next: a dynamic contact-angle descriptor (β0 correction of Lee et al. [4]); calibrated conformal intervals; rebound and contact-time targets.
