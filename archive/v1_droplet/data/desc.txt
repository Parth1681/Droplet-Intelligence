File 1: Rebound Data
Description:              This file contains a table consisting of 1498 water-glycerol
                          droplet impact experiments on monolayer-functionalized laser-
                          textured aluminum samples, where each row represents a single
                          measurement and includes the following parameters:
                          a) sample name,
                          b) droplet diameter (in m),
                          c) droplet impact velocity (in m s-1),
                          d) density of the droplet (in kg m-3),
                          e) surface tension of the droplet (in N m-1),
                          f) dynamic viscosity of the droplet (in Pa∙s),
                          g) distance between adjacent laser beam passes (in μm),
                          h) approximate average depth of laser-made channels (in μm),
                          i) Reynolds number (dimensionless),
                          j) Weber number (dimensionless),
                          k) droplet-surface contact time (in seconds),
                          l) rebound efficiency (dimensionless),
                          m) droplet maximum spreading factor (dimensionless), and
                          n) maximum lamella velocity during the spreading phase
                             (in m s-1).

Type of data:             Processed data.

Data format:              Data in .csv.

How data were acquired:   The superhydrophobic surfaces were prepared on aluminum
                          alloy (1050A H24, ≥99.5% Al) plates via two-step fabrication
                          process comprising laser texturing and hydrophobization. A
                          nanosecond faber laser system (FL Mark-C with JPT Opto-
                          electronics "M7 30 W" MOPA source) was used to fabricate 6
                          and 25 μm deep microchannels. The laser beam was scanned in
                          a crosshatch pattern with a center-to-center distance of 50, 100,
                          200, 400, 600 and 800 μm distance between successive passes.
                          Following laser texturing, the surfaces were hydrophobized by
                          drop-casting a 3 mM solution of 1H,1H',2H,2H'-
                          perfluorododecil-1-phosphonic acid (FDPA) in isopropanol.

                          Droplet impact measurements were conducted using water-
                          glycerol binary mixtures with 0, 20, 60, 78 and 91 wt.%
                          glycerol. The mixtures were infused through a blunt G27
                          needle with a syringe pump (Harvard Apparatus PHD 2000)
                          operated at a constant flow rate of 150 μL min-1 to produce
                          droplet of controlled size. The droplets were released onto the
                          samples from five different heights to vary the impact
                          velocities. Droplet impact was captured using a high-speed
                          optical camera (Photron FASTCAM Mini UX100) equipped
                          with a macro lens (Laowa 100mm f/2.8 2x Ultra Macro APO)
                          at a frame rate of 5000 fps and spatial resolution of
                          ~12 μm px-1. A white LED backlight (Thorlabs SOLIS-2C

                                            3
                        powered by a DC2200 controller) was used for illumination.
                        Prior to each measurement, the temperature was recorded using
                        a SparkFun SHTC3 (Qwiic) sensor and a SparkFun RedBoard
                        Qwiic Arduino controller.

Data source location:   Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, University of Ljubljana,
                        Aškerčeva cesta 6, 1000 Ljubljana, Slovenia.

                        The data were acquired in the period between Apr – Dec
                        2024.

File 3: Contact angles - Water
Description:              The file contains a table presenting the average measured static
                          and dynamic contact angles of water droplets on monolayer-
                          functionalized laser-textured aluminum samples featuring 6
                          and 25 μm deep microchannels. The center-to-center distances
                          between adjacent microchannels are approximately 50, 100,
                          200, 400, 600 and 800 μm.

                          The table’s columns list sample names and the rows list the
                          corresponding static, advancing, and receding contact angles,
                          and contact angle hysteresis.

Type of data:             Processed data.

Data format:              Data in .csv.

How data were acquired:   Static contact angles were measured using a goniometer
                          (Ossila) by depositing water droplets onto the surfaces and
                          observing the resulting contact angle. Each measurement was
                          repeated 5 times. Processing of the videos was performed in
                          Ossila’s proprietary software.

                          Advancing and receding contact angles were measured using
                          the droplet inflation/deflation method on a custom-made
                          experimental setup for goniometry equipped with a CMOS
                          camera and a strong LED backlight. A syringe pump was used
                          to inflate and deflate the droplets while the movement of the
                          triple contact line across the surface was recorded with the
                          camera. Processing of the videos was performed using custom-
                          developed algorithms in MathWorks MATLAB environment.

Data source location:     Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, University of Ljubljana,
                          Aškerčeva cesta 6, 1000 Ljubljana, Slovenia.

                          The data were acquired in the period between Apr – Dec
                          2024.

Description:              The file contains a table presenting the average measured static
                          contact angles of water-glycerol droplets with 91 wt.% glycerol
                          on monolayer-functionalized laser-textured aluminum samples
                          featuring 6 and 25 μm deep microchannels. The center-to-
                          center distances between adjacent microchannels are
                          approximately 50, 100, 200, 400, 600 and 800 μm.

                          The table’s columns list sample names and a single row lists the
                          corresponding static contact angles.

Type of data:             Processed data.

Data format:              Data in .csv.

How data were acquired:   Static contact angles were measured using a goniometer
                          (Ossila) by depositing droplets of water-glycerol binary
                          mixture with 91 wt.% glycerol onto the surfaces and observing
                          the resulting contact angle. Each measurement was repeated 5
                          times. Processing of the videos was performed in Ossila’s
                          proprietary software.

Data source location:     Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, University of Ljubljana,
                          Aškerčeva cesta 6, 1000 Ljubljana, Slovenia.

                          The data were acquired in the period between Apr – Dec
                          2024.

File 5: Advancing contact angles - Water-glycerol mixtures
Description:              The file contains a table presenting the average measured
                          advancing contact angles of water-glycerol droplets with 20,
                          60, 78 and 91 wt.% glycerol on monolayer-functionalized laser-
                          textured aluminum samples featuring microchannels with
                          depths of 6 and 25 μm. The center-to-center distances between
                          adjacent microchannels are approximately 50, 200 and 800 μm.

                          The table’s rows list sample names and the corresponding
                          advancing contact angles for the water-glycerol mixtures in the
                          following other: 20, 60, 78 and 91 wt.% glycerol.

Type of data:             Processed data.

Data format:              Data in .csv.

How data were acquired:   Advancing contact angles were measured using the droplet
                          inflation method on a custom-made experimental setup for
                          goniometry equipped with a CMOS camera and a strong LED
                          backlight. A syringe pump was used to inflate the droplets
                          while the movement of the triple contact line across the surface
                          was recorded with the camera. Advancing contact angles were
                          measured using water-glycerol binary mixtures with 20, 60, 78
                          and 91 wt.% glycerol. Processing of the videos was performed
                          using custom-developed algorithms in MathWorks MATLAB
                          environment.

Data source location:     Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, University of Ljubljana,
                          Aškerčeva cesta 6, 1000 Ljubljana, Slovenia.

                          The data were acquired in the period between Apr – Dec
                          2024.

Folder 1: SEM images of the test surfaces
Description:              The folder contains 39 scanning electron microscopy (SEM)
                          images of the monolayer-functionalized laser-textured
                          aluminum samples featuring microchannels with depths of 6
                          and 25 μm and center-to-center distances of approximately 50,
                          100, 200, 400, 600 and 800 μm, and the monolayer-
                          functionalized smooth aluminum sample. Each sample is
                          imaged at 43×, 100× and 350× magnification.

                          The name of each image is composed of the sample name and
                          the magnification of the image.

Type of data:             Raw data.

Data format:              Data in .tif.

How data were acquired:   SEM imaging was performed using a JCM-7000 NeoScope
                          SEM, utilizing the secondary electron detector and operating at
                          an accelerating voltage of 15 kV.

Data source location:     Faculty of Mechanical Engineering, University of Ljubljana,
                          Aškerčeva cesta 6, 1000 Ljubljana, Slovenia.

                          The data were acquired in the period between Apr – Dec
                          2024.
