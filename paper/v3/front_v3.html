Droplet Intelligence: physics-informed Gaussian-process prediction of maximum spreading on laser-textured surfaces never seen in training
Dimensionless inputs, surface-held-out validation, calibrated uncertainty, and image and video measurement front ends
Parth Sharma · Thapar Institute of Engineering & Technology, Patiala, India

Abstract

The maximum spreading ratio βmax = Dmax/D0 of an impacting drop sets how much surface a drop wets, yet data-driven models of it are usually scored on random splits in which every surface is also in training. We ask the design question instead: how accurately can βmax be predicted on a textured surface that has never been seen? Using a public dataset of 1,498 impacts on 12 laser-textured aluminium surfaces and 125 on a smooth hydrophobic plate (REF-H), five water–glycerol mixtures and Re = 8.9–4,435, We = 8.0–119, we represent each impact by ln Re, ln We, ln D0 and two texture descriptors, and evaluate every model by leave-one-surface-out (LOSO) cross-validation with a paired bootstrap over surfaces. A Matérn-3/2 Gaussian process (GP) reaches a LOSO RMSE of 0.0400 (R² = 0.991, MAE = 0.0242), against 0.0563 for XGBoost on identical inputs (difference interval [+0.005, +0.028]) and 0.250 for the Laan scaling law alone; 12 other learners ranked below the GP. On REF-H, never used for training or selection, the GP scores 0.0591. Its 90% intervals, calibrated on cross-validated residuals, cover 90.1% of held-out textured impacts but only 61.6% on REF-H, failing mostly for low-viscosity drops. Several alternatives scored lower RMSE (dimensional inputs 0.0372, a Laan-backbone residual GP 0.0388), but none differed significantly from the baseline. Adding ln Oh changes nothing because ln Oh = ½ ln We − ln Re; contact-angle inputs degrade REF-H. The texture fraction φ can be read from a 43× SEM image with no loss of accuracy (LOSO 0.0397), and a video pipeline recovers D0, V and βmax of synthetic test clips to within 1.9%. The same GP runs in a browser in about 2 ms per query.

Keywords: drop impact; maximum spreading; laser-textured superhydrophobic surfaces; Gaussian process regression; dimensionless analysis; leave-one-surface-out validation; uncertainty calibration; SEM image analysis; high-speed video
